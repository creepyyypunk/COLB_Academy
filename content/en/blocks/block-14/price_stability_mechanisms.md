Several types of stablecoins have emerged, each with their own price stability mechanism. Whilst the market for stablecoins is expected to continue to grow in size, challenges remain in ensuring stability and trust, both essential for enabling mass adoption.


## Algorithmic stablecoins

In the absence of tangible assets for use as collateral, algorithmic stablecoins rely on a combination of mathematical rules  and incentives to regulate the price of the stablecoin. Whilst this is an innovative approach, with clear potential, the mechanism creates technical vulnerabilities and exposes stablecoins to instability in the DeFi ecosystem, especially during periods of market stress.

## Crypto-backed stablecoins

Crypto-backed stablecoins are backed by a reserve of cryptocurrencies, held in a collateral pool. If this falls below a certain threshold, users may be required to add more collateral or risk position liquidation in order to maintain the stability of the stablecoin. In recent years, these assets have experienced significant drops in value, highlighting the fragility of crypto-backed stablecoins when market conditions are unfavourable.

## Fiat-backed stablecoins

The most prevalent of the three, fiat-backed stablecoins are collateralised with a reserve of fiat currency held in a bank. Despite their relative stability, these stablecoins are vulnerable to issues surrounding the underlying fiat currency, such as bank runs or devaluation. The primary concerns linked to fiat-backed stablecoins are that of run-risk and redeemability, which principally depend on the composition and management of the reserves. Likewise, the absence of a comprehensive regulatory framework could impact the functioning of current stablecoin issuers. On top of that, they are largely custodial, meaning that reserves are held by a centralised entity, raising the risk of unilateral decision-making. Furthermore, the lack of transparency around reserve holdings could lead to speculation about the quality of a stablecoin's collateral, which could include credit, specific investments and rate risks.
